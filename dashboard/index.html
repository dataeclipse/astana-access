Astana · 1 January 2026

How far is school, on foot, for a child in Astana?

The city grew from 1.35 to 1.64 million people in three years. This atlas measures the walk from every home to every state school and preschool, using official capacity, enrolment and population, and asks where new schools should stand.

Scroll to read. The map follows the text; drag or zoom it at any time.

01

The norm met on the map is not met on foot

The planning standard asks for a school within 500 m. Drawn as a circle, it is met for most children. Walked along streets, around fenced courtyards and across highways, it is met for about one child in five.

02

Access is lowest where the city grows fastest

Accessibility per child weighs every school within 1.5 km by its capacity and by the children who share it. Red blocks have less than the city average, blue more; outlined groups are significant clusters of low access.

Yet over nine tenths of the inequality lies inside districts, between blocks a few hundred metres apart.

03

Schools are full as well as far

Design capacity counts places in one shift. Most state schools teach in two shifts, and many enrol more pupils than two full shifts can hold.

04

The 2026–2028 plan answers crowding more than distance

The city’s construction department plans 25 school projects. Its 17 new schools go mostly to the districts where schools are fullest, not to the blocks where children have no school nearby.

05

The land is already reserved, on paper

The city’s detailed planning projects hold 238 sites for new schools. Choosing the 17 that reach the most children without a school would reach far more children than the plan.

06

By 2028 the children move to the new estates

A forecast of new housing, trained on what was built in 2020–2026, places the children that migration adds by 2028 in the estates being built. The plan already follows the cranes: most of its new schools stand next to construction sites. Yet many of these children will still have no school within walking distance.

07

Preschools: places exist, but not near every child

There are more preschool places than children attending, and the waiting list is almost entirely children under two. The shortage is one of location.
