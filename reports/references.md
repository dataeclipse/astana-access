## How every entry was verified

No entry was typed from memory.

1. **Journal articles, books and ACM papers** were resolved against Crossref by DOI or by title, first author and year together, and the BibTeX was taken directly from `doi.org` content negotiation. A record was accepted only when title, first author and year all matched. 61 entries.
2. **NeurIPS papers** have no DOI. Their BibTeX was taken from the official files on proceedings.neurips.cc. The official records carry no page numbers, so none were added. 3 entries.
3. **Theil (1967)** has no DOI. Publisher, place, series and volume were confirmed against WorldCat.
4. **Legal acts, standards, datasets and documentation** were checked against the source page: title, number and date of the act, dataset index, URL, access date.
5. **Content claims.** Every statement Chapter 1 makes about a specific recent or less familiar paper was checked against that paper's abstract, retrieved from OpenAlex or Crossref. Where no abstract is openly available, the text claims no more than the title states: Liang et al. (2020), Wadoux et al. (2021), Neutens (2015).

Field hygiene: e-journal article numbers are stored as `eid`; publication year follows the print issue, not the online-first date; all-caps titles and nonstandard hyphens from Crossref were normalised; month fields and ISSNs were dropped.

**Placeholder authors resolved.** The four entries previously listed as Anonymous now carry their real author lists: Gu, Li and Zheng (2025); Sun et al. (2025, Tianjin); Başeğmez, Doğan and Aydın (2025); Atakhanova and Baigaliyeva (2025, Astana). Earlier project documents stated five placeholders; the correct number, checked against the committed file, is four.

**Wrong year corrected.** Lucas, van Wee and Maat carried the year 2015, which is the online-first date; the print issue is volume 43 of 2016.

**Page numbers not invented.** NeurIPS does not assign page numbers, and Crossref holds none for Rey and Anselin (2007). Those entries are left without pages rather than filled from memory.

**Unsupported claims removed from Chapter 1.** Sentences that had no source in the bibliography were rewritten or cut:

- a claim that studies in China used the Theil decomposition across administrative scales;
- a list of SHAP applications to urban heat, land surface temperature, urban vitality and urban expansion, replaced by Li (2022) and Li (2024), which address spatial effects directly;
- a mention of deep neural networks for school site selection;
- the statement that post-Soviet urban renewal is explicitly identified as under-researched, which came from a web article; replaced by a characterisation of the Astana scholarship actually cited, and a narrower claim that no fine-grained quantitative accessibility study was found;
- vendor review claims about ArcGIS Urban, replaced by the verified capabilities of the ArcGIS location-allocation solver.

**A factual correction in Section 1.7.** The earlier draft stated that all compared platforms are commercial. UrbanSim is not, and ArcGIS does include a capacitated coverage model. The comparison now says so, and the stated gap was narrowed accordingly: the gap is not the absence of location models but the absence of demand estimation, accessibility measurement, equity diagnosis and anchoring to the national standard around them.
