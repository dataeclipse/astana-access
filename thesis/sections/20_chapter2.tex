\chapter{Data and Methodology}
\label{ch:methods}

This chapter describes how the framework turns open data into measures of access to schools and preschools, and how each hypothesis of Section~\ref{sec:hypotheses} is tested. Every number reported here can be reproduced from the sources of Table~\ref{tab:sources} by a single command of the accompanying software, described in Section~\ref{sec:implementation}.

\section{Study Area and Scope}
\label{sec:study-area}

Astana, the capital of Kazakhstan, had 1,639,176 residents on 1 January 2026, an increase of 7.2 per cent in one year \cite{astanapop2026, statages2026}. Growth is concentrated in the districts of recent construction: in 2025 the population of Nura rose by 23 per cent and that of Esil by 9 per cent, while Baikonur lost 4 per cent. Births follow: in 2025 Nura recorded 6,103 births, more than any other district, while births in Baikonur fell from 4,648 in 2019 to 2,707 \cite{statbirths2026}. The city is divided into six districts, of which Saraishyk was separated from Almaty by a joint decision of the akimat and the maslikhat of 23 May 2024 and named on 27 August 2024 \cite{saraishyk2024, saraishykname2024}; the Bureau has recomputed the population of all districts in their present boundaries from 2022.

The object of the analysis is the provision of state general schools that admit pupils by catchment, and of preschool organisations of every ownership listed in the municipal enrolment register. Private schools are excluded because they charge fees and do not serve a catchment, except the six Binom schools, which are financed by state order and to which the education department assigns service territories like state schools \cite{catchments2026}; private preschools are included because places in them are allocated through the same municipal queue as places in state preschools, and they hold 56 per cent of attending children \cite{indigo2026}.

All spatial data are stored in WGS~84 and processed in UTM zone 42N. Results are aggregated to the hexagonal grid of the H3 system at resolution 9 \cite{h3, sahr2003}, whose cells cover about 0.1\,km$^2$, small enough to resolve a walking catchment and large enough to hold a stable population estimate.

\section{Data Sources and Provenance}
\label{sec:sources}

The framework was designed under the constraint that no result may wait for data requested from a state body: every estimate is first built from sources that anyone can obtain. Information requests were nevertheless sent, and the replies of the education department, the statistics bureau and the construction department, which arrived late in the work, are used to test those estimates, to correct the list of schools and to replace the floor area of schools by their design capacity. The code of this work fetches only sources whose \texttt{robots.txt} allows it; the pages of the preschool register were saved by hand from a browser, and the cards of its organisations and the layer of detailed plans of the city geoportal were compiled by members of the project team.

\section{Facility Inventory and Record Linkage}
\label{sec:inventory}

\textbf{Schools.} OSM objects tagged as schools were merged with the state school register by record linkage \cite{fellegi1969, christen2012}. Names were normalised, school numbers and types extracted, a pairwise similarity combining number, type, name tokens and distance was computed, and pairs were assigned one to one by the Hungarian method \cite{kuhn1955}. Against a manually labelled sample, the frozen rules reached a precision of 0.977, a recall of 0.944 and an F1 score of 0.960. The supply layer holds the 117 schools that the education department assigns a service territory \cite{catchments2026}: 110 numbered state schools linked between the register and OSM, the Daryn lyceum and six Binom schools financed by state order, of which one absent from OSM is placed from its 2GIS listing. Five unnamed school grounds of 1.1 to 3.4\,ha mapped more than 150\,m from any known school are added as presumed new schools; three of them are identified with programme schools in Section~\ref{sec:programme-list}. The official list of schools \cite{schoolregister2026} matches 120 entries of the layer by number or, for the Binom schools, by name; the state entries left over are two boarding lyceums for gifted pupils, a specialised lyceum, an evening school, an international school and two schools without a service territory. Its addresses geocode to a median of 25\,m from the positions in the layer; of the eight more than 400\,m apart, six are confirmed at the layer position by their 2GIS listing, and the list gives schools 25 and 36 one address.

\textbf{Official service territories.} \label{sec:catchments} In reply to an information request, the education department supplied the lists of addresses assigned to each of the 121 schools with a service territory \cite{catchments2026}. The lists are free text: streets with house numbers, ranges, whole streets, settlements and housing complexes, interleaved with notes on classes taught in Russian or Kazakh. They were parsed into 8,938 entries, of which 8,272 single houses, 254 ranges, 373 whole streets and 39 named places, and geocoded with the gazetteer; 77 per cent of single houses resolve exactly, from 51 per cent in Saraishyk to 88 per cent in Almaty, and ranges and whole streets add the address points of OSM on those streets within the school's district. Snapped to dwellings within 25\,m, the lists assign 13,764 dwellings to 115 schools. They replace the 11 catchments of the enrolment portal as the test of how schools are actually used.

\textbf{Preschools.} The municipal register lists 790 organisations, 148 state and 642 private, attended by 66,402 children, but its list gives no coordinates, and attendance only on the card of each organisation. Addresses were resolved with a gazetteer built from OSM address points and street names, including Kazakh, Russian and former names, so that a street renamed since the address was registered is still found. A coordinate is accepted only when the house number matches exactly. 653 organisations, 82.7 per cent, were placed this way or through the OSM object of the same numbered state preschool; 75 were placed approximately and 62 not at all. Against the OSM buildings of 70 state preschools, exact matches lie a median of 9\,m away, and 98.6 per cent within 150\,m.

\section{Population Surface and Test of H1}
\label{sec:population}

No population figure is published below the district level, so the population of each H3 cell has to be estimated. Dasymetric methods distribute a known total over an ancillary surface \cite{mennis2003, eicher2001}; the question is which surface.

\textbf{Dwellings.} Of the 93,507 building footprints, only 4,755 carry a type in OSM. Residential use is therefore predicted by a LightGBM classifier \cite{ke2017} from footprint geometry, mapped floors, address points, land use and the density and size of neighbouring buildings. Positive labels come from typed residential buildings and from houses named in polling-station territories and school catchments; negative labels from typed non-residential buildings and buildings on industrial, commercial or institutional land. Under spatial cross-validation with 1\,km blocks \cite{roberts2017} the classifier reaches a ROC-AUC of 0.982 and an F1 score of 0.922; trained without any house-list label, it recovers 80.5 per cent of the 2,663 untyped buildings that the house lists prove residential. A third check comes from 2GIS listings of residential complexes \cite{twogis2026}: 1,584 of them fall inside a footprint, 91.7 per cent of these are classed as dwellings, and among the 551 decided by the model alone the recall is 77.9 per cent. Missing floor counts of large buildings are imputed from the seven nearest tagged buildings, with a leave-one-out error of 2.0 floors. Against the floors listed in 2GIS the error is 1.9 floors where OSM carries a tag but 4.4 floors where the count is imputed, two floors too low on average, because new complexes rise above their older neighbours. Replacing the imputed counts of the 438 affected buildings by the listed ones reweights dwellings within their stations but leaves the district ranking of accessibility unchanged and moves no district by more than 0.003 of the city mean; the worst-served fifth of children keeps 99 per cent of its cells.

\textbf{Polling stations.} The akim's decision forming polling stations \cite{pollingstations2025} assigns every address of the city to one station, either by a list of houses or by a walk along named streets. The text was parsed into 386 territorial stations; walks were turned into polygons by intersecting the named streets in order, and houses were located with the gazetteer. 340 stations received territories, and 246 of 247 reconstructed polygons fall in the district the decision names.

\textbf{Allocation.} Within each official reporting unit $u$ with population $P_u$, station $s$ receives a share proportional to $F_s^{\alpha}$, where $F_s$ is the residential floor area of its dwellings, and within the station each dwelling $b$ receives a share proportional to its weighted floor area $f_b$:
\begin{equation}
P_b = P_u \, \frac{F_s^{\alpha}}{\sum_{s' \in u} F_{s'}^{\alpha}} \, \frac{f_b}{F_s}, \qquad f_b = A_b \, n_b \, h_b,
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
where $A_b$ is the footprint, $n_b$ the number of floors and $h_b$ equals 2.15 for houses and 1 for apartment blocks. The factor $h_b$ comes from the ratio of official living area to measured gross floor area for each building type \cite{statconstruction2026}. With $\alpha = 0$ every station in a unit holds the same population; with $\alpha = 1$ the station level disappears and floor area alone decides.

\textbf{Test of H1.} Each candidate surface is scaled to the city total and compared with the six official district figures for 1 January 2026 \cite{statages2026}. Figure~\ref{fig:h1} shows the ratio of predicted to official population. WorldPop misallocates by 26.0 per cent on average and residential floor area by 27.5 per cent, in opposite directions: WorldPop overstates the established Baikonur district by 76 per cent, floor area overstates the new districts, where housing is not yet fully occupied. The number of polling stations misses by 9.2 per cent. H1 is supported. The final surface uses $\alpha = 0.2$, which fits the district totals best (10.0 per cent), and every later result is recomputed with $\alpha = 0$, with floor area alone and with WorldPop.

\textbf{Age structure.} The official count of residents by single year of age in each district \cite{statages2026} gives the share of children aged 1 to 6, the preschool cohort, and 7 to 17, the school cohort, which is applied to every cell of the district. The school cohort agrees with enrolment: 310,892 children aged 7 to 17 lived in the city on 1 January 2026, and 311,282 pupils attended day schools in the 2025--2026 school year \cite{stateducation2026}. WorldPop, used for this purpose in an earlier version of the model, assigns the whole city one age structure, 12.8 per cent preschool and 18.6 per cent school age in every district. The official shares range from 7.3 per cent preschool children in Baikonur to 14.5 per cent in Esil, and the difference changes the results for preschools (Section~\ref{sec:results-h2}). The official split between Almaty and Saraishyk on 1 January 2026 is unusual, 19.8 and 8.1 per cent preschool children, whereas the same series gives 14.2 and 13.7 per cent for 1 January 2023; the two districts are therefore given their combined shares, 14.7 per cent preschool and 21.2 per cent school-age children, which the enrolment of their schools supports (Section~\ref{sec:results-h2}), and results under the official split are reported alongside.

\section{Supply Measures}
\label{sec:supply}

Design capacity, enrolment and shifts of every state and Binom school come from the official list of schools \cite{schoolregister2026}: 162,344 places for 273,340 pupils in the 2025--2026 school year, 1.7 pupils per place, with 109 of the 120 schools teaching in two shifts. Design capacity is the baseline supply of schools. Floor area measured from OSM, the supply of earlier versions of the model, correlates with capacity at only 0.64 (Spearman); it is kept, with equal weight per school, as an alternative in the sensitivity runs, and it estimates capacity for the three schools of the layer without an entry. For preschools, the design capacity and the number of attending children of every organisation are published on its card in the register; the cards of all 794 organisations were compiled by members of the project team and the supervisor between 12 and 15 October 2026 \cite{indigocards2026}. They count 73,419 places, 29,939 in state and 43,480 in private organisations, and 66,574 attending children, within 0.3 per cent of the totals the register printed on 30 September, and agree with the register on ownership and address for every organisation. Design capacity is the baseline supply of preschools; the number of attending children and equal weight are the alternatives.

\section{Accessibility Model}
\label{sec:access-model}

\textbf{Network.} Every OSM way with a highway tag inside the city, except motorways, ways under construction and ways closed to pedestrians, forms an undirected graph of 152,491 nodes and 5,934\,km \cite{boeing2017}. Walking distance from each dwelling to each facility within 4\,km is computed by Dijkstra's algorithm, adding the straight-line gaps from building and facility to their nearest nodes. Over 206,000 pairs more than 300\,m apart, the network distance is never shorter than the straight line, and is a median 1.41 times longer.

\textbf{Normative coverage.} The share of children with a facility within the statutory radius, 500\,m for schools and 300\,m for preschools \cite{sprk2013}, is computed both along the network and in a straight line, since the standard does not say which is meant.

\textbf{Floating catchment.} Accessibility per child follows the enhanced two-step floating catchment area method \cite{luo2003, luo2009} with one change. In the original first step a child counts towards every facility within the catchment, so that demand is multiplied in dense areas and children beyond the catchment load nothing. Here each child is allocated once among the facilities within a wider allocation radius $d_L$, in proportion to the distance weights, in the spirit of the selection weights of the three-step method \cite{wan2012}:
\begin{equation}
R_j = \frac{S_j}{\sum_i D_i \, \dfrac{W_L(d_{ij})}{\sum_{k} W_L(d_{ik})}}, \qquad
A_i = \sum_{j:\, d_{ij} \le d_0} R_j \, W(d_{ij}),
\label{eq:e2sfca}
\end{equation}
where $S_j$ is the supply of facility $j$, $D_i$ the number of children at dwelling $i$, and $W$ a distance decay that is Gaussian in the baseline and linear or a step in the sensitivity runs. The baseline uses a walking catchment $d_0$ of 1.5\,km, about 20 minutes, with an allocation radius $d_L$ of 3\,km for schools, and 1\,km with 2\,km for preschools. The choice was tested against two official sources, the enrolment of 119 schools and the service territories of 115 (Section~\ref{sec:results-access}).

\section{Inequality and Cluster Diagnostics}
\label{sec:equity-methods}

The distribution of $A_i$ over children is summarised by the Gini coefficient and by Theil's index \cite{theil1967}, which decomposes exactly into a part between districts and a part within them:
\begin{equation}
T = \underbrace{\sum_g s_g \ln \frac{\bar A_g}{\bar A}}_{\text{between}} + \underbrace{\sum_g s_g T_g}_{\text{within}},
\label{eq:theil}
\end{equation}
where $s_g$ is the share of total accessibility held by the children of district $g$, $\bar A_g$ its mean and $T_g$ the index within it. Spatial clustering is measured by global Moran's $I$ \cite{moran1950} and by local Moran statistics \cite{anselin1995} on H3 cells weighted by children, with the six adjacent cells as neighbours and 999 conditional permutations; significant low-low cells at the 5 per cent level are the deficit clusters.

\textbf{Test of H3.} Each analysis is repeated 72 times per facility type, crossing four population surfaces, three supply measures, three decay functions and two catchment sizes. Stability is judged by the rank correlation of district means with the baseline and by the overlap of the worst-served fifth of children, measured by the Jaccard index of the cells concerned.

\section{Models of Revealed Demand and Siting}
\label{sec:siting-methods}

\textbf{Preschool vacancy.} The register publishes free places only after the evening distribution to waiting lists, so an organisation with published places had no waiting list that day. Whether an organisation had free places is modelled from its own characteristics, among them its design capacity, and from these plus the demand around it: children within 500\,m and 1\,km, preschools within the same radii, and children per place of all preschools within them. Logistic regression and a regularised LightGBM are compared under spatial cross-validation with 2\,km blocks, five folds repeated five times; models are explained with SHAP values \cite{lundberg2017, lundberg2020}.

\textbf{Reconstruction of 2023.} Programme sites were chosen in 2022 and 2023, so the city is rebuilt as it was on 1 January 2023: OSM buildings mapped by that date are read through the history interface of the Overpass API, dwellings not overlapping them are removed, and the official population of 1 January 2023, recomputed by the Bureau for the six districts within their present boundaries \cite{statages2026}, is allocated by Equation~\eqref{eq:allocation}, with the age shares of the same date.

\textbf{Test of the first part of H4.} Candidate cells are the 957 H3 cells with at least 20 school-age children in 2023. Each programme school marks its nearest cell. Two scores are computed from the state before the programme: the deficit, the number of children with no state school within walking distance who would gain one from a school in the cell, and the density, all children within the same distance. Their ability to single out the programme cells is compared by ROC-AUC with bootstrap intervals, an appropriate summary when only 23 of 957 cells are positive \cite{saito2015}.

\section{Location Optimisation}
\label{sec:optimisation-methods}

The counterfactual to the programme is the maximal covering location problem \cite{church1974, murray2016} with capacities \cite{current1988}. With binary site variables $x_j$ and the share $z_{ij}$ of the uncovered children $w_{ij}$ of demand unit $i$ within walking distance $d_0$ of site $j$ served by $j$:
\begin{equation}
\max \sum_{i,j} w_{ij} z_{ij} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
\sum_j w_{ij} z_{ij} \le W_i, \quad
\sum_i w_{ij} z_{ij} \le C_j x_j, \quad
\sum_j x_j = k, \quad
x_j \in \{0,1\}, \; 0 \le z_{ij} \le 1,
\label{eq:mclp}
\end{equation}
where $W_i$ is the number of uncovered children in unit $i$, $C_j$ the capacity of site $j$ and $k$ the number of programme schools. Capacity is the number of seats times two shifts. Demand units are H3 cells at resolution 10. The problem is solved exactly with the HiGHS solver.

Candidate sites are constrained by land. A raster of 10\,m cells marks as blocked every building, road, water body, railway, park, forest, cemetery, industrial site, allotment, construction site and institution mapped in 2023. A site is feasible if a disc of 3.8\,ha, the plot that the standard prescribes for a 2,000-seat school \cite{sprk2013}, fits on free land within 600\,m of a candidate cell; the school is then placed on that plot. The realised programme is compared with the optimum with and without the land constraint, with the 23 most populous cells, and with 200 random choices of 23 cells.

\section{Implementation and Testing}
\label{sec:implementation}

The framework is a Python package organised in twelve stages, from acquisition of boundaries to the figures of this chapter and the next, each callable separately and all reproducible by one command. It relies on GeoPandas, SciPy, LightGBM, SHAP and the HiGHS solver; every stochastic step is seeded. Ninety unit tests cover address parsing and geocoding, the parsing of polling-station territories, population allocation, the accessibility index, the inequality measures, the standard's land rules, the optimisation models, the parsing of official service territories and of the official list of schools, and the checks against external listings. The architecture is documented in UML in Appendix~A and the test results in Appendix~B.
