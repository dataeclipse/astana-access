\chapter*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}

The work developed a data-driven framework for assessing spatial accessibility to social infrastructure and evaluating its siting, and applied it to state schools and preschool organisations in Astana using open data, checked against official replies to information requests. The four hypotheses were tested as follows.

\textbf{H1 is supported.} Population below the district level was reconstructed from the official totals of districts, the territories of 386 polling stations described in an akim's decision, and 93,507 building footprints classified by a gradient-boosting model. At district level this surface misses the official population of the six districts by 10 per cent, against 26 per cent for WorldPop and 28 per cent for residential floor area alone. Children are counted with the official age structure of each district rather than the single structure that WorldPop assumes for the whole city. The choice matters downstream: the cells that WorldPop marks as worst served overlap those of the building-based surfaces by only 13 per cent.

\textbf{H2 is supported in part.} Nura, the district that grows fastest, has the lowest accessibility to both schools and preschools, 77 per cent of the city mean for each, and Esil, the other growing district, is second lowest for both, 82 and 87 per cent, and holds the largest cluster of blocks with low access to schools. But districts differ little: 91 per cent of the inequality in access to schools and 95 per cent in access to preschools lies within districts, and the deficit is a matter of blocks. Four corrections of the data changed the district picture on the way: the official age counts removed an apparent preschool deficit in Baikonur, the official list of service territories added seven schools that serve catchments, six of them in Esil and Nura, official design capacity, replacing floor area, moved Esil from 92 to 82 per cent of the mean for schools, and the design capacity of preschools moved it from 94 to 87 per cent for preschools. Schools are also crowded: the official list counts 1.7 pupils per design place, and 49 per cent of pupils attend schools beyond what two shifts can hold, most of all in Saraishyk. Read as a walking distance, the statutory radius of the planning standard is met for 22 per cent of school-age and 35 per cent of preschool-age children; read as a straight line, for 64 and 78 per cent. The official service territories send four children in ten past a nearer school, and only 20 per cent live within 500\,m of the school they are assigned to.

\textbf{H3 is supported at the level of cells.} Across 72 specifications per facility type the worst-served fifth of children falls in the same cells under every building-based population surface, with a Jaccard overlap of about 0.8, but not with WorldPop. District rankings are stable for schools, with Nura worst in most runs, and for preschools only when supply reflects the size of organisations.

\textbf{H4 is supported in direction in its first part and partly in its second.} Against the city of 2023, the availability of a free plot singles out the 23 sites of the Comfortable School programme in the official list (ROC-AUC 0.71); the deficit of school places within walking distance does so more weakly (0.59) and the density of children not at all (0.42), and the preference for deficit over density, positive in 96 to 98 per cent of bootstrap samples, is borderline. Without a land constraint, 23 optimally placed schools would have reached 3.4 times as many uncovered children within 1\,km as the programme did; on plots that were free in 2023, 19 per cent more within 1\,km and 66 per cent more within 1.5\,km. School capacity hardly limited coverage. The programme beat a density rule and most random choices, 66 per cent at 1\,km and 96 per cent at 1.5\,km. The city's construction plan for 2026--2028, received in reply to an information request, does not follow the deficit: its 17 located new schools reach 15 per cent of the 76,542 children without a school within 1\,km, the deficit singles out their sites no better than density (ROC-AUC 0.54 and 0.55), and in Esil, the district with the largest deficit, it falls by less than 4 per cent; the plan follows crowding by district instead. The detailed plans of the city already contain 238 school sites, and the 17 of them that reach the most children without a school would reach 2.6 times as many as the plan.

\textbf{Objectives.} All six objectives were met. An inventory of 117 schools with a service territory and 790 preschools was assembled and its positions validated to a median error of 9 to 24\,m; the population surface was built and tested; accessibility and compliance were computed; inequality and clusters were described; revealed demand for preschool places was modelled, with design capacity as the strongest predictor and demand pressure in the expected direction but not yet distinguishable from zero; the programme was tested against capacitated optimisation; and every conclusion was tested for robustness. The weakest links are the single snapshot of preschool vacancies and the waiting list, known only for the city as a whole.

\textbf{Paths to deployment.} The framework runs from public data with one command, with the official replies used as checks, and can be rerun each year, when the statistical bureau publishes district totals and the polling-station decision is revised. For the city's education department it can provide a monitoring layer of walking access and statutory compliance by block, and a screening tool that ranks candidate plots by the uncovered children they would reach. The clearest lesson for planning is to reserve land for schools in districts under construction before the housing around it is finished, since the plots that were free in 2023 had largely been built over by 2026, and to build first the reserved sites that reach the most children without a school.

\textbf{Further development.} The estimates would improve most from administrative data that remain unpublished: the waiting list for preschools by district and year of birth, and population by address or by polling station. Capacity, enrolment and shifts by school were received in reply to an information request and are used here. Repeated snapshots of the preschool register would turn the vacancy model into a test of demand. Public transport and winter walking conditions can be added to the impedance, and the same framework can be applied to other facilities the planning standard regulates, such as polyclinics and sports facilities, and to other cities of Kazakhstan.
