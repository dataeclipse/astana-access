\chapter*{Conclusion}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Conclusion}

The work developed a data-driven framework for assessing spatial accessibility to social infrastructure and evaluating its siting, and applied it to state schools and preschool organisations in Astana using only open data. The four hypotheses were tested as follows.

\textbf{H1 is supported.} Population below the district level was reconstructed from the official totals of districts, the territories of 386 polling stations described in an akim's decision, and 93,507 building footprints classified by a gradient-boosting model. At district level this surface misses the official population of the six districts by 10 per cent, against 26 per cent for WorldPop and 28 per cent for residential floor area alone. Children are counted with the official age structure of each district rather than the single structure that WorldPop assumes for the whole city. The choice matters downstream: the cells that WorldPop marks as worst served overlap those of the building-based surfaces by only 14 per cent.

\textbf{H2 is supported for schools and not for preschools.} Accessibility to state schools in Esil and Nura, the districts of recent growth, is 70 and 72 per cent of the city mean, and the largest deficit clusters lie there. For preschools Nura is also the lowest, at 83 per cent, but districts differ little and 97 per cent of the inequality lies within them; the deficit is a matter of blocks. The apparent preschool deficit of Baikonur under a uniform age structure disappears with the official age counts. Read as a walking distance, the statutory radius of the planning standard is met for 21 per cent of school-age and 35 per cent of preschool-age children; read as a straight line, for 61 and 78 per cent.

\textbf{H3 is supported for schools and, at the level of cells, for preschools.} Across 48 specifications the worst-served school district was always Nura or Esil, and district rankings correlated with the baseline at 0.94 in the median. Preschool rankings of districts are not stable, since the districts are close, but the worst-served cells recur across building-based population surfaces for both facility types. They do not recur with WorldPop.

\textbf{H4 is supported in its first part and partly in its second.} Against the city of 2023, the sites of the Comfortable School programme are singled out by the deficit of school places within walking distance (ROC-AUC 0.62 to 0.63) and not by the density of children (0.44 to 0.45), with moderate evidence given 21 sites, and land availability is the strongest predictor. Without a land constraint, 21 optimally placed schools would have reached 3.0 times as many uncovered children within 1\,km as the programme did; on plots that were free in 2023, 12 per cent more within 1\,km and 55 per cent more within 1.5\,km. School capacity never limited coverage. The programme beat a density rule and 81 to 89 per cent of random choices.

\textbf{Objectives.} All six objectives were met. An inventory of 111 state schools and 790 preschools was assembled and its positions validated to a median error of 9 to 24\,m; the population surface was built and tested; accessibility and compliance were computed; inequality and clusters were described; revealed demand for preschool places was modelled, with a result in the expected direction but not yet distinguishable from zero; the programme was tested against capacitated optimisation; and every conclusion was tested for robustness. The weakest links are the proxies for facility capacity and the single snapshot of preschool vacancies.

\textbf{Paths to deployment.} The framework runs from public data with one command and can be rerun each year, when the statistical bureau publishes district totals and the polling-station decision is revised. For the city's education department it can provide a monitoring layer of walking access and statutory compliance by block, and a screening tool that ranks candidate plots by the uncovered children they would reach. The clearest lesson for planning is to reserve land for schools in districts under construction before the housing around it is finished, since the plots that were free in 2023 had largely been built over by 2026.

\textbf{Further development.} The estimates of capacity and population would improve most from administrative data that the work deliberately did without: enrolment and design capacity by school, attendance by preschool, and population by address. Repeated snapshots of the preschool register would turn the vacancy model into a test of demand. Public transport and winter walking conditions can be added to the impedance, and the same framework can be applied to other facilities the planning standard regulates, such as polyclinics and sports facilities, and to other cities of Kazakhstan.
