\textbf{Statutory compliance.} Table~\ref{tab:compliance} and Figure~\ref{fig:compliance} give the share of children living within the radius of the planning standard. Read as a straight-line radius, the standard is met for 60 per cent of school-age children and 76 per cent of preschool-age children. \textbf{Do families use the nearest school?} The official catchments of 11 schools \cite{sakura2026} assign 2,988 located houses. The assigned school is the nearest by walking distance for 57.3 per cent of them and one of the two nearest for 67.8 per cent; the median walk to the assigned school is 1,133\,m, and for one house in ten it exceeds 2.8\,km. Three schools follow the nearest rule almost exactly and others hardly at all. Catchments are administrative, not geometric, which is why the accessibility index below lets a child reach every school within walking distance rather than assigning each child to the nearest one.

For schools, H2 is supported. Esil and Nura, where 38 and 46 per cent of residential floor area was built after 2022, have 73 per cent of the city's accessibility per child, against 114 and 138 per cent in Baikonur and Saryarka. The largest deficit clusters lie in Esil, which holds 4.4 per cent of all school-age children inside significant low-low clusters, followed by Nura with 2.0 per cent (Figure~\ref{fig:access-school}). Removing the five presumed new schools lowers Nura further, to 70 per cent of the mean, so their inclusion does not create the result.

For preschools, H2 is rejected. The deficit lies in the established districts: Baikonur reaches 64 per cent of the mean and Saryarka 79, while Almaty reaches 159 per cent and the new districts lie near the mean (Figure~\ref{fig:access-preschool}). Private organisations, which hold most places, appear to have opened where new housing created demand, leaving the older districts dependent on the state network; the register gives no opening dates, so this explanation remains a hypothesis.

In both cases nine-tenths of inequality lies within districts and only one-tenth between them (Equation~\eqref{eq:theil}), and accessibility is strongly clustered. Planning that compares district averages, the form in which provision is usually reported, misses most of the inequality the cell-level surface reveals. Figure~\ref{fig:lorenz} shows the Lorenz curves.

H3 holds at the district level under every specification and at the cell level across the building-based surfaces. It does not hold at the cell level when population comes from WorldPop: the cells it marks as worst served overlap those of the building-based surfaces by only 14 per cent. Supply measure, decay function and catchment size move individual cells; the choice of population surface moves the deficit itself. This is the practical consequence of H1: an assessment that takes its population from the standard gridded product would direct new facilities to different places.

\section{Backtest Against the National School Construction Programme: Test of H4}
\label{sec:results-h4}

\subsection{The programme as built}
\label{sec:programme-list}

Of the 24 sites announced in 2023 \cite{skc2023}, 23 were reported built \cite{skc2024} by September 2025, 16 in 2024 and 7 in 2025, and one was still in budget preparation in April 2026. Ten public sources allow 21 of the 23 to be located: 15 with high confidence, through the published school number or an address that resolves to it, and 6 with medium confidence. Two sites, announced only by street codes absent from OSM, could not be located. The 21 located schools offer 38,400 places in one shift.

\subsection{Were sites chosen by deficit or by density?}

Against the city of 1 January 2023, the deficit score singles out the programme cells with a ROC-AUC of 0.63 at 1\,km and 0.64 at 1.5\,km, while density scores 0.46 and 0.45, no better than chance. The difference is positive in 98.7 and 97.5 per cent of bootstrap samples, with 95 per cent intervals from $+0.02$ to $+0.29$ and from $0.00$ to $+0.34$. The strongest single predictor is the availability of a plot: 13 of the 21 sites had a feasible plot in 2023, against 29 per cent of all cells (ROC-AUC 0.67). The first part of H4 holds: the programme went where children lacked a school within walking distance and where land was available, not where children were most numerous.

Table~\ref{tab:backtest} and Figure~\ref{fig:programme} compare the programme with the optimum of Equation~\eqref{eq:mclp}. Three results stand out. Capacity never binds: no school receives more uncovered children within walking distance than its 4,000 places in two shifts, and the capacitated optimum equals the uncapacitated one. Land explains most of the distance from the theoretical optimum: without a land constraint 21 schools could have reached 2.7 times as many uncovered children within 1\,km, but on plots that were actually free the best choice reaches 11 per cent more at 1\,km and 48 per cent more at 1.5\,km. And the programme did well within the land it had, beating the density rule by a wide margin and 95 to 97 per cent of random choices.

The second part of H4 therefore holds against the unconstrained optimum and only partly against the land-feasible one. The planning lever with the largest effect is not the choice among available plots but the reservation of plots in the right places before housing is built around them.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{A data-driven framework without official microdata.} The work set out to assess access to social infrastructure using only open data, and the main methodological finding is that this is feasible when the ancillary sources are chosen for what they encode rather than for their resolution. Polling-station territories encode the distribution of registered residents, are published as law, and change with the city. Building footprints encode where people can live but not whether they do. Satellite-derived population grids encode the past. Combined in a hierarchy anchored to official totals, the three give a surface that reproduces district populations within 9 per cent, and the choice matters for every downstream result.

\textbf{Measurement choices that change conclusions.} Three choices changed results by large factors: straight-line against walking distance, by a factor of two to three in statutory compliance; the first step of the floating catchment method, which multiplied demand until it was checked against observed enrolment; and the land constraint, which shrank the apparent room for better siting from 2.7 times to 11 per cent within 1\,km. In each case the more careful choice was the one that could be checked against an observation.

\textbf{Implications for planning in Astana.} Schools are the binding problem in the new districts and preschools in the old ones, so a single district-level indicator of provision misleads in both. Within districts, deficits cluster in identifiable groups of blocks, which suggests that siting can be targeted at a finer scale than the district. The national programme behaved better than a density rule would have and close to the best that free land allowed within 1\,km; the larger gains lie in reserving land early, since plots that were free in 2023 had largely been built over by 2026.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Population below the district level is an estimate; equal shares per polling station are an assumption supported by district totals, not an observation, and 46 territorial stations have no reconstructed territory. Age structure is taken from WorldPop. School capacity is known for 11 schools and preschool attendance only as class means, so supply measures are proxies, and results are reported for two of them. The planning standard was read in its edition of July 2021; the current edition of June 2025 must be checked before its values are cited as current. Two of the 23 programme sites could not be located and six are located with medium confidence. Land ownership is unknown, so free land is land without buildings, not land available for a school. The vacancy label comes from a single evening.
